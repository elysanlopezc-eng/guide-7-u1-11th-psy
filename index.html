Target Grade 11th Grade / 4th High School
10 Minutes

Criteria for Analyzing Psychological Cases

Learn how to systematically apply multiple psychological perspectives to analyze real and fictional scenarios.

1

Identify Observable Behavior

Clearly define the subject's actions, emotional responses, and social patterns without introducing immediate assumptions.

2

Select Primary Lenses

Choose two contrasting perspectives (e.g., Cognitive vs. Behavioral, Biological vs. Sociocultural) that best fit the situation.

3

Formulate Explanations

Use core terms for each perspective (e.g., schemas, reinforcement, neurotransmitters, cultural norms, or adaptation).

4

Propose Intervention

Suggest how treatment or support strategies would differ based on the selected perspectives.

Perspective Quick Reference Chart

Biological

Genes, brain chemistry, nervous system, hormones.

Cognitive

Thought patterns, memory, perception, problem-solving.

Behavioral

Conditioning, rewards, punishments, learned habits.

Psychodynamic

Unconscious conflicts, childhood trauma, defense mechanisms.

Humanistic

Free will, self-actualization, ideal vs. actual self.

Sociocultural

Cultural norms, group pressure, social environment.

Evolutionary

Survival value, natural selection, species traits.

Biopsychosocial

Holistic integration of biology, psychology, and social factors.

Case Profile 1: Sofia's Screen Addiction

Sofia, a 16-year-old high school student, spends over 6 hours every night scrolling through social media feeds. She reports feeling anxious when her phone is away, experiences disrupted sleep, and has seen her academic performance decline over the past semester.

Behavioral Explanation

Intermittent reinforcement (notifications and likes) triggers dopamine rewards, creating habit loops. Negative reinforcement occurs when scrolling removes feelings of boredom or loneliness.

Cognitive Explanation

Sofia holds irrational beliefs, such as "If I am not updated constantly, I will lose my friends" (FOMO). Her cognitive schema misinterprets missed posts as social rejection.

Clarifying Overlapping Perspectives

How do Cognitive and Behavioral perspectives differ when treating anxiety?

Behavioral therapists focus on exposure therapy and altering environmental triggers/reinforcers. Cognitive therapists focus on identifying distorted thoughts (catastrophizing) and replacing them with balanced thoughts (cognitive restructuring).

What is the main difference between Humanistic and Psychodynamic approaches?

Psychodynamic theory looks backward to childhood and unconscious urges, assuming behavior is driven by internal conflicts. Humanistic theory looks forward at conscious choice, personal potential, and free will.

Can a single behavior have Biological, Cognitive, AND Sociocultural causes?

Yes! This is the core principle of the Biopsychosocial Model. Most complex human behaviors (like depression, addiction, or aggression) result from an interaction among biological traits, mental processes, and social environments.
